---
title: Constraints make style
tag: arts
source: cognite-original
---

A sonnet has fourteen lines. A blues verse has a familiar twelve-bar spine. A haiku counts beats. These are not handcuffs so much as games with rules. Constraints reduce the search space so a maker can finish, compare, and surprise inside a shared form.

Twentieth-century artists often invented their own constraints: a painter limited to three colors, a filmmaker shooting in one room, a novelist avoiding a letter. The Oulipo writers treated restriction as an engine. The point is not suffering. The point is that infinite choice is a poor collaborator.

Beginners sometimes wait to "feel free" before they start. Practitioners pick a box. Write 300 words. Use only photographs from one walk. Compose for a cheap keyboard. The box creates style because style is a pattern of decisions, and decisions need edges.

There is a warning attached. A constraint that you never chose — a feed's length, a template's layout, a client's panic — can also produce a style, just not yours. The artistic move is to notice which limits are instruments and which are someone else's default. Then steal the useful ones on purpose.
