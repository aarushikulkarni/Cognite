title: Attention as a moral problem
tag: philosophy
source: cognite-original
---

Simone Weil wrote that attention is the rarest and purest form of generosity. Iris Murdoch argued that moral life is less about heroic choices than about what we steadily look at. Both are pushing against a picture of ethics as a calculator you take out only in emergencies.

If that is right, then the design of your day is ethical design. What you repeatedly notice becomes easier to care about. What you never look at becomes abstract, including other people's inner lives. A feed that trains you to skim faces as content is not only a productivity issue.

This does not require a hair shirt. Attention is finite, so some filtering is sanity. The philosophical question is who sets the filter. If an interface always chooses the next object for you, you are practicing a kind of passivity. If you choose a book, a problem, or a conversation and return to it after wandering, you are practicing a kind of freedom — not metaphysical freedom, just authorship of the next few minutes.

Cognite's product bet sits here, not in neuroscience theater. A short reading is a small claim: you can pick an object of attention on purpose. Whether that makes you a better person is a longer argument. Whether it is possible is something you can test this afternoon.
