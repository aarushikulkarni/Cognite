---
title: The examined life is a method
tag: philosophy
source: cognite-original
---

Socrates, as Plato presents him, walks around Athens asking people what they mean by courage, justice, or piety. The point is not to win a quiz. It is to show that confident definitions often collapse when you test them with counterexamples. "The unexamined life is not worth living" is easy to quote and hard to practice, because examination threatens status.

Socratic method, stripped of marble statues, is a loop: state a claim, ask what it implies, look for a case that does not fit, revise. It is closer to debugging than to preaching. You can use it on a political slogan, a career plan, or the sentence "I do not have time to read."

Two mistakes follow the slogan. One is endless doubt that never chooses an action. The other is treating examination as a personality — being the person who always asks a clever question at dinner. The useful middle is local: pick one belief that actually guides your week and pressure-test it.

Philosophy here is not a rival to science. It is care with concepts. If you cannot say what you mean by "focus," "waste," or "a good day," you will still live by some definition. It will just be an unexamined one, usually borrowed from whatever interface is nearest.
